Land Surface Temperature Analysis

This multi-temporal spatial analysis traces how surface thermal dynamics have evolved across a changing landscape over a 15-year period from 2009 to 2024. Maps like these do crucial heavy lifting in urban studies and environmental monitoring—they make the invisible impact of land-use changes immediately visible, showing exactly how landscape transformations dictate local surface temperatures over time.

What makes this sequence particularly compelling is how clearly it captures the dynamic tug-of-war between natural cooling mechanisms and built-environment pressures. The central water body stands out as a reliable climate buffer, remaining remarkably cool across every timeframe and proving just how essential natural hydrological features are for moderating regional temperatures. On the flip side, we see a steady creeping expansion of thermal hotspots, particularly along the northern and southern edges. These growing warm zones tell a familiar story of rapid development: as vegetation gives way to concrete, asphalt, and denser infrastructure, localized surface heat builds up. By looking at these shifts side by side over fifteen years, the layout pinpoints where environmental stress is concentrating, giving planners the spatial evidence they need to design smarter green infrastructure, protect vulnerable ecologies, and build more heat-resilient communities.

Urban Thermal Field Variance Index

This series of maps provides a multi-temporal spatial analysis of the Urban Thermal Field Variance Index (UTFVI) across Hyderabad for the years 2009, 2014, 2019, and 2024. By categorizing thermal comfort and surface temperature variation into five distinct zones—ranging from "Excellent" and "Good" (green) to "Moderate" (yellow), "Bad" (orange), and "Worst" (red)—the maps map the evolving intensity and geographical spread of the Surface Urban Heat Island (SUHI) effect over a 15-year period.

The significance of this temporal assessment lies in identifying zones of severe thermal stress caused by rapid urbanization, loss of green cover, and expanding built-up surfaces. Tracking the expansion of high-UTFVI zones ("Bad" and "Worst") alongside preserved cooler areas ("Excellent") gives urban planners, researchers, and decision-makers the empirical data needed to target thermal mitigation, direct sustainable land management, and implement climate-resilient urban infrastructure.

Ecological Comfort / Discomfort Zonation

This layout illustrates a multi-temporal spatial mapping of the Ecological Comfort/Discomfort Zone within the study area across four benchmark years: 2009, 2014, 2019, and 2024. By isolating areas designated as the "Comfortness Zone" (highlighted in light blue), these maps delineate the spatial extent and temporal continuity of microclimatically favorable zones over a 15-year period..

The significance of tracking this ecological comfort zone lies in assessing environmental stability and microclimatic resilience amidst ongoing urban expansion. Monitoring the shrinkage, fragmentation, or shifts in these comfortable regions provides critical insight into localized thermal dynamics, aiding urban planners and researchers in evaluating ecological health, preserving essential cool refugia, and developing sustainable climate-adaptation strategies.

Forest Fragmentation & Change Detection

This series of maps depicts a spatiotemporal change detection analysis of forest cover dynamics across four distinct time intervals: 1995–2005, 2005–2015, 2015–2025, and an overall 30-year assessment from 1995–2025. By categorizing surface modifications into Degeneration (red), Regeneration (green), and Unchanged (cream) zones, the layout tracks the spatial progression of forest loss, gain, and structural continuity across the region.

The significance of this multi-decadal evaluation lies in quantifying long-term ecosystem trajectories, habitat fragmentation, and forest degradation driven by anthropogenic pressures and natural factors. Identifying persistent zones of degeneration alongside successional recovery offers critical spatial insights for forest management authorities, aiding in the design of targeted reforestation programs, habitat corridor restoration, and sustainable land-use governance.
